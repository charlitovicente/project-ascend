# Engineer Manifesto

## Why do I build software?

I build software because I enjoy understanding how problems work and turning them into systems that help people.

When I started out, I was excited by trends and new technologies. As I grew, I realized I enjoy something deeper: analyzing a situation, designing a better approach, and seeing how that solution helps the client or end user.

What motivates me most now is the challenge itself. I like thinking through the problem, finding a cleaner solution, and learning enough to become better every time I do it.

## What kind of engineer do I want to become?

I want to become a reliable engineer who can be trusted on every project.

I also want to be the kind of engineer who can give useful advice, especially when I have already faced the same kind of problem before.

## What problems excite me the most?

The problems that excite me the most are the ones where I can understand how the system works, choose the right tools, and decide on the best approach with confidence.

## What kind of leader do I want to be?

I want to be a leader people follow because they trust my judgment.

I want to be reasonable, calm under pressure, and consistent in the way I lead.

## What values will I never compromise?

Respect, honesty, responsibility, and willingness to keep learning.

## What should my teammates say about me?

I want them to say that I am reliable, easy to work with, and someone who can be counted on when the work becomes difficult.

## What should clients remember me for?

A person who was easy to talk to, good at solving problems, and careful in the way I handled their needs.

## What will success look like in 10 years?

Success in 10 years means I am still building systems that matter, leading teams when needed, and working on software that continues to be useful even as technology changes.

It also means I have grown into someone people trust when things are difficult.

## My Engineering Mission

My engineering mission is to keep learning the technologies that will matter in the future, while staying grounded in real problem-solving, good judgment, and respect for the people I work with.
